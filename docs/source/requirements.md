## Page 3 — Choose One. Build It. Prove It Works.

> Pick one of four problem statements and build a working web or mobile prototype for it.
>
> THE BRIEF — Code it. Run it live. This is a software challenge. Your solution is an app, web app or tool
> that you code and demonstrate working, not slides or a concept.
> No data or input is provided, before or during your presentation.
>
> YOUR MISSION — 01 Choose: Pick one of the four problem statements and decide which user and problem you
> will focus on. 02 Build: Code a working prototype that covers the five core expectations. Bonus items are

> and impact.
>
> Each team presents for 10 minutes and is scored out of 100.

## Page 7 — Problem Statement 3 of 4 · Supply Chain and Logistics: Order-to-Delivery Control Tower

> CONTEXT — Plant X, a fictional polymer producer, ships product in bulk to business customers. An order
> passes through planning, dispatch, transport and delivery, yet customers and sales teams often learn an
> order is late only after it already is.
>
> OBJECTIVE — Build a web app that tracks orders from plant to customer, predicts delays, and gives every
> stakeholder a clear, shared view.
>
> WHAT WE ARE EXPECTING
> 1. Separate logins for the customer, the logistics coordinator and the transporter or driver.
> 2. Track orders and shipments on a map, with status milestones.
> 3. Predict an ETA and delay risk for each shipment, and show the reason.
> 4. Alert the customer and coordinator on delay risk, with a suggested action such as reroute or split shipment.
> 5. Dashboard of on-time performance and delay causes by route and transporter.
>
> BONUS — Truck slot booking at the plant gate · "Where is my order?" chatbot · Upload and download of
> invoice and proof of delivery
>
> Hypothetical scenario. Plant X is a fictional company.

## Page 9 — Five Ground Rules

> 1. One statement per team — Choose one problem statement and cover all five core expectations. Bonus items are optional.
> 2. Build with any stack — Web or mobile, any language or framework. Open-source tools and APIs are welcome.
> 3. Create your own sample data — Fictional data only. No real, personal or confidential data, and none is supplied to you.
> 4. Run your own demo — Judges give no inputs or scenarios. You choose and run your own end-to-end flow live.
> 5. Generative AI is allowed. Declare it. — State where and how you used it. Undeclared use costs 10 points.
>    You must be able to explain every part of your code, data and design.

## Page 10 — Your 10 Minutes

> Each team gets one 10-minute session with the judges.
>
> PROBLEM & APPROACH ~2 min — Users, problem, key decision, where AI is used and where it is not.
> LIVE DEMO ~4 min — Your scenario run end to end on your own sample data.
> DEFEND ~2 min — Controls, risks, assumptions, pilot or next step.
> JUDGES' Q&A ~2 min — At least one technical and one assumption question.
>
> HAVE THESE READY — A measurable impact: At least one indicator, with the calculation and the assumptions
> behind it. A simple architecture: A diagram of how your solution is built, matching what you demo.
> Your controls: How you handle AI risk: human review, confidence, privacy and access.

## Page 11 — How Teams Will Be Scored

> Ten criteria, 100 points in total.
>
> | Criterion | Points |
> | --- | --- |
> | 1. Problem understanding & user fit | 10 |
> | 2. Innovation & original thinking | 10 |
> | 3. Working prototype / functionality | 20 |
> | 4. Technical design & architecture | 10 |
> | 5. Feasibility & scalability | 10 |
> | 6. Business / user value & impact | 15 |
> | 7. Responsible AI, security & data | 10 |
> | 8. User experience & accessibility | 5 |
> | 9. Presentation & demo | 5 |
> | 10. Q&A & team understanding | 5 |
> | TOTAL | 100 |
>
> Working prototype carries the most weight (20), then impact (15).

## Page 12 — Final Checklist

> Run through this with your team before your session.
> - One statement chosen, all five core expectations covered
> - Your focus user and problem named
> - Working prototype ready to run live
> - Own sample data created, nothing real or confidential
> - Impact indicator with calculation and assumptions
> - Architecture diagram that matches your demo
> - Human review, confidence and privacy controls built in
> - Generative AI use declared
> - Every member can explain code, data and trade-offs
>
> Build. Demo. Defend.
